# Sookie's Canvas Press

Published version: https://claude.ai/artifact/4SmmxXf5kBFZEC8BpBSXF1

A browser tool for printing needlepoint designs straight onto blank canvas. It replaces the CanvasTrue workflow with one where every scan stands on its own and everything can be edited.

## How it works

1. **Design**: add artwork (PNG, JPG, PSD or PDF). Each file becomes a layer, and you can set each layer's opacity, size and position before the layers are flattened into one picture. Pick the mesh (10, 12, 13, 14 or 18) and the size in stitches or inches (or a common size like 5 × 7 in), choose how many colors, and convert. Then edit stitch by stitch: paint, fill, pick color, erase, change any color, merge colors, undo and redo. **Read a painted canvas** takes a photo, scan or shop picture of a canvas that's already painted. It measures the mesh, finds every intersection, takes the painted color at each one, and turns bare canvas into unprinted stitches. It also cleans up the blurred in-between colors a photo leaves where two colors meet. That gives you an editable design you can print on a new canvas. The **Original** view shows the picture you read, with the stitch frame on it. The **Original picture on top** slider fades it over your stitches so you can compare. Designs show as **Canvas mesh** (threads and holes, like a real canvas) or **Squares**. **Move stitches** shifts the whole design, and the original moves with it. **Open stitch file** loads a Stitchly file (.stitchly) with its exact DMC colors and traced picture, a stitch image (one pixel per stitch, or an app export where each stitch is a block, with or without grid lines, PNG or JPG), a chart PDF, or an image with one pixel per stitch, or an OXS chart, with no color changes. You can save a stitch image (PNG) or a printable chart (PNG).
2. **Scan**: print the Carrier sheet once. By default it has only four corner dots and thin corner marks, so it uses almost no ink. For the scan, put black paper behind the canvas or scan with the lid open so the holes look dark. A solid black window is optional. Tape the canvas inside the corner marks and scan the whole page at 300 or 600 dpi. The app finds the four dots and then every thread intersection, all from that one scan. There is no calibration sheet and no earlier scan it depends on. If the scan is upside down, it gets turned around. Teal points were found clearly, amber points were faint, and red points were guessed from their neighbors. Drag any point to fix it, or use arrow keys. **Next to check** steps through the doubtful points inside the design area. **Snapshot** shows the scan exactly as it came in, and you can save it.
3. **Print**: move the design on the canvas, boost the colors for canvas (richness, deepen, contrast, ink reach), and save a letter-size PDF at 300 or 600 dpi. Print it at 100% / Actual Size on the Carrier. **Preview** shows the design ink over your real threads. The printer nudge (in 1/200 inch steps) and a plain-paper test page are there if a printer lands off.

Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, and there are no credits.

## How the alignment works

The four Carrier dots are printed by your own printer, so their spots in the scan link scan pixels to the printer's page. Each intersection the app finds is mapped through those dots onto the page. Each stitch is then drawn as a filled patch that runs out to the centers of the four holes around its intersection. Because the dots come from the same scan as the canvas, nothing carries over from an earlier scan.

## Opening it

Open `index.html` in Chrome, Edge or Safari, or use the published version.

Reading a PDF uses pdf.js from cdnjs. If that can't load, the app pulls the scanned image out of the PDF itself. That works for most scanner PDFs.

## More tools

- **My designs**: save designs by name (with their original picture and initials) and open them later to keep editing. Save changes updates the open design; Save as new keeps both. Designs are stored on the device and, on claude.ai, also in your own private storage in your Claude account, so they survive the browser clearing its storage and appear on your other devices. The design you are working on is kept there too. Back up all downloads every design as one file and Open a backup loads them anywhere.
- **Design file**: Download design file (or "Design file (opens to edit)" under Save the design) saves one design as a `.sookie.json` file with its stitches, colors, mesh, initials and original picture. Open a design file, or Open stitch file, puts you straight back in the stitch screen.
- **Big Move button** on the picture switches to moving the view and back.

- **DMC colors**: every color shows its closest DMC floss number. **Pick DMC** chooses an exact DMC color, **Add a DMC color** adds one, and **Change all colors to their closest DMC** converts the whole design. **Replace all with** shows color swatches with their DMC numbers.
- **Color key (PDF)**: a printable sheet with the design picture, each design color beside its closest DMC swatch, DMC number and name, and stitch counts. It's on the Design step (Save the design) and the Print step.
- **Initials**: on the Print step, **Add initials** opens a pop-up for the letters, font, height, color (design colors or any DMC) and position. By default they go bottom right, 2 inches below the design. They print with the design and appear in Preview and the color key.
- **Text**: a tool on the Design step. Pick the words, font, height and color in the pop-up, click on the design to place it, then rotate (15° or 90°), make it smaller or bigger, or drag it before clicking Done to stitch it in. Text past the edge grows the design frame.
- **Convert after reopening**: if the artwork list is empty (after a reload or opening a saved design or design file), Convert to stitches rebuilds it from the design's saved original picture.
- **Conversion style: Art or Photo**: Art (the default) reads the picture at full detail, picks the art's own flat colors rather than blends, gives each stitch the color covering most of it, keeps thin dark outlines and tidies lone stray stitches, so plaids, stripes and outlined drawings stay crisp. Photo blends each stitch's pixels for smooth shading.
- **Side by side**: a view button that shows the original picture on the left and the stitches on the right at the same zoom, moving together. Tap the picture to pick its color: it selects the matching palette color, or adds the new color, ready to paint on the right.
- **Circle guide**: on the Design step, a round outline drawn over the design on screen only (never printed) to check an ornament is round. Sizes 3-5 in or any diameter; 4 in on 13 mesh reproduces the 13-4 Circle Stitchly chart stitch for stitch (52 across, 144 around). Stitches poking outside the circle are marked with a pink ×; the guide can be moved a stitch at a time, centered, or stitched in with the selected color.
- **Shapes**: a library of motifs (heart, star, bow, flower, dot, diamond, tree, snowflake, moon, leaf, holly, gift, paw, ornament, candy cane) in the Edit tools and quick toolbar. Each starts at a real-life size converted to stitches for the current mesh (a 1-inch heart is 13 stitches tall on 13 mesh), in the selected color; make it Bigger or Smaller, turn, flip, drag, tap a color in its toolbar to recolor, then Done.
- **Blank canvas**: on the Design step (Artwork), Blank canvas asks for width and height in inches and starts an empty stitch grid at the current mesh with ten starter DMC colors, for drawing your own design. The previous design is kept in Earlier designs.
- **Show or hide colors**: under the color list, each color has a checkbox to hide it on screen (it shows as a faint ghost), plus Show all and Only selected. Hidden colors still print.
- **Ask Claude**: on the Design step, type a change in plain words ("make the background light blue", "add a 2-stitch red border", "clean up stray stitches") or a question ("which DMC for skin?"). Claude gets the stitch grid, the colors with DMC numbers and a picture of the design, and returns exact stitch changes the app applies; Undo takes them back. With a Select box active, only that area changes. Uses the viewer's own Claude usage and asks permission the first time.
- **Select & copy**: a tool on the Design step and in the quick toolbar. Drag a box around part of the design, then Copy, Cut, Delete or Select all. A copy floats over the design: drag it into place, turn it 90° or flip it, and tap Done. Paste places another copy. Bare spots in a copy stay see-through, so it doesn't erase what's under it, and a copy past the edge grows the design frame. On a computer, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X, Ctrl+V and Delete work too.
- **Arrow pad**: when something can be moved (a floating copy or text, the design with Move stitches, or a scan point you tapped on the Map), arrow buttons appear above the Move button. Each tap moves it one stitch (a scan point moves a small step); hold an arrow to keep going. A floating copy can also be dragged from anywhere on the picture, and scan points are easier to grab with a finger.
- **Canvas area**: Show canvas area draws the bare canvas border (2 in by default) around the design, labeled with both the design size and the canvas size to cut.
- **Finishes**: the size list includes common finishes (mini key fob 1.2 × 1.2 in, bookmark 1 × 6 in, passport cover, luggage tag, card holder, coin purse, eyeglass case, coaster, wristlet, clutch, tray insert, stocking, ornament, pillow), sized to the inside stitch area. Save your own finisher's sizes as "My finishes". **Make copies** repeats a design in a grid with gaps so one canvas holds several minis.
- **Background removal from the full picture**: for artwork, Remove background finds the background in the original picture at full detail and rebuilds the stitches from what's left, so edges keep their true colors and thin parts survive. It stays removed when colors or size change; Bring the background back undoes it.
- **Crop and remove background**: on the Design step. Crop is a tool: drag a box over the part to keep. Remove background clears every color along the outside edge plus any backdrop that covers the whole picture (stripes, patterns, a background behind a border), the blurry fringe, leftover specks and stray bits of a border. A strength slider controls how many shades go, and an option clears gaps inside the design. **Magic erase** clears any tapped patch of similar color.
- **Point numbers and rulers on the Map**: every amber or red point has a number that stays the same after it is fixed, and the design's columns and rows are numbered along the top and left, counted like a chart from the design's top-left stitch. The selected point's number, column and row show under the picture, and Go to point number / Go to column, row jump straight to a spot.
- **Real thread count**: the Scan step reports the canvas's measured holes per inch across and down (measured on the page through the Carrier dots), and the Print step shows the design's true printed size on that canvas. Printing always follows the actual intersections, so the count only changes the finished size, never the alignment. Fit to exact size on this canvas changes the stitch count to the measured threads so the design prints at its planned size or its finish size (re-reading the picture if it has not been hand-edited, otherwise stretching the stitches); Undo puts it back.
- **Auto mesh**: the Scan step measures the canvas mesh and warns when it doesn't match the mesh the design is sized for, or the mesh you picked.
- **Photo canvas mode**: on the Print step, choose Stitch-painted (one clear color per intersection) or Photo. Photo prints the design's original picture itself, with smooth shading, carried onto the real threads through the scan and kept only on the design's stitches (outline stopped short next to bare canvas). With Match the photo's colors to my pattern (on by default), each stitch's area is shifted so its average is that stitch's pattern color, keeping the picture's light and dark detail inside it, so the canvas agrees with the color key; the Print step reports how many stitches land within a close match. Needs a design made from artwork; stitch files and blank canvases print stitch-painted.
- **Color dots print mode**: on the Print step, Color dots prints a round dot of each stitch's color on its thread crossing (size adjustable, 10-45% of a stitch) as a guide for painting the canvas by hand, using about a fifth of the ink of a full print. Cheat sheet (PDF) prints one page to paint from: the painted look beside the dots, a lettered close-up of the busiest spot, and each color's letter, paint swatch, closest DMC and stitch count.
- **Paper copy of my scanned canvas**: on the Print step (Printer nudge and test page), a dress rehearsal without canvas. It prints the scanned canvas on plain paper, mapped through the Carrier dots so the threads land exactly where the real canvas sits on the Carrier (threads gray, holes white, little ink). Load that paper like the Carrier and print the design PDF on it to see whether the ink lands on the threads.
- **Clean edges next to bare canvas**: where a stitch borders bare canvas, its ink stops just past its own crossing instead of running halfway down the thread toward the unpainted neighbor, so stitchers can tell which crossings are painted. Where two colors meet, they still meet halfway along the thread. Adjustable on the Print step (Color for canvas); at 100% the paint still covers the whole crossing. **Edge test** prints three small blocks with different edge settings on a scrap of canvas so you can pick the one that reads best.
- **Calibration sheet**: like a printer or Cricut alignment page, with no scanner needed. Print Step 1 on plain paper, put the same sheet back in like the Carrier, print Step 2, and read the numbers where the two rulers' lines meet in a straight line (across and up/down). Set my nudge applies them.
- **Clean color-to-color edges**: where two clearly different colors meet, each is printed short of the middle of the thread between them, so the ink spread meets on that thread instead of tinting the neighbor's crossing. Near-identical shades still meet halfway so areas stay solid.
- **Ink spread learning**: when you check a printed canvas with the blank scan of the same canvas still loaded, Check my print compares the two (lined up through the Carrier dots), measures how far the paint ran past the design outline on each side, and offers to print those edges shorter so the paint lands on the edge crossings. Map and Preview keep showing where the paint should end up.
- **Check my print**: at the end of the Print step, load a scan of a printed canvas (on its Carrier). The app measures where the ink landed on the threads and suggests a printer nudge if it's off by more than 1/10 of a stitch.

DMC color data: [craft-color-codes](https://www.npmjs.com/package/craft-color-codes) by [MakeBead](https://makebead.com/), CC BY 4.0. DMC doesn't publish screen colors, so all DMC matches are approximate; check against real floss.
