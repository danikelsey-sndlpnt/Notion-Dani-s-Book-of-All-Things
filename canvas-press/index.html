Artwork

Read a painted canvas takes a photo, scan or listing picture of a canvas that's already painted. It finds every intersection and turns its color into a stitch you can edit and print on a new canvas.

PNG, JPG, PSD or PDF. Each file becomes a layer, and the layers are flattened into one picture. Open stitch file takes a Stitchly file (.stitchly, with its DMC colors), a stitch image (one pixel per stitch, or an export where each stitch is a block, grid lines are fine), a chart PDF, or an OXS chart, and keeps its colors.

Ask Claude

Claude sees your stitch grid, colors (with DMC numbers) and a picture of the design, then changes the stitches. Undo takes back anything it did. If you've selected part of the design with Select, only that part changes. Uses your own Claude usage.

Edit stitches

Background removed. It stays removed when you change colors or size.

Text: pick the words, font, size and color, then click on the design where the text goes. It becomes regular stitches you can edit. Press Esc to stop placing.

Crop: drag a box over the part you want to keep. Remove background clears every color that runs along the outside edge, plus the blurry fringe and loose specks, so those stitches print as bare canvas. Raise Background strength to clear more shades, lower it to protect colors close to the background. Magic erase: tap any leftover patch to clear it and everything touching it in a similar color. Undo brings any of these back.

Move stitches: drag the design to shift it. The original picture moves with it, so you can see what you moved. The frame grows if stitches go past the edge.

Save the design

The design file is the best way to keep a design: open it with Open a design file (or Open stitch file) and you're right back in the stitch screen with everything as you left it. The stitch image has one pixel per stitch.

Carrier sheet

Print once on plain letter paper at 100% / Actual Size and reuse it as long as the four corner dots stay clean. The dots tell the app exactly where the page sits in the scan, so each scan stands on its own.

The scan needs a dark background behind the canvas so the holes show up. Best reusable setup: print this sheet on cardstock, glue black construction paper or cardstock flat inside the corner marks, and cover each dot with matte tape (not shiny packing tape). Leave the black paper on for scanning and printing, since ink that goes through the holes lands on black. Other options: scan with the lid open, or print the solid black window (uses a lot of ink).

On the Map, each amber or red point has a number, and the design's columns and rows are numbered along the top and left (counting from the design's top-left stitch, like a chart). Tap a point and its number, column and row show at the bottom of the picture.

Amber and red points are spots where the scan was faint (tape, a shadow, a fold). Fix them automatically looks closely at the scan around each one and snaps it onto the real crossing. Only points inside your design matter for printing; ones outside are ignored.

Show design (above the picture, on the Map) puts your design's colors on the canvas so you can see where each stitch lands while you move points.

Dragging moves around the scan. To fix a point, tap it to select it (it gets a ring), then drag it onto its intersection or use the arrow buttons. Only the selected point moves, so you won't grab points by accident. Arrow keys move it one pixel. Drag the purple corner dots if the app missed one.

Line up my printer (calibration sheet)

Like a printer or Cricut alignment page. 1. Print Step 1 on plain paper at 100% / Actual Size. 2. Put that same sheet back in the printer exactly the way you load the Carrier, and print Step 2 on it. 3. On each ruler, find the number where the lines from Step 1 and Step 2 meet in one straight line (a phone camera zoomed in helps). Pick those numbers below: the BIG rulers (top of the sheet and far left) give the rough amount, the small rulers in the middle give the exact step. If two or three neighbors look equally straight, pick the middle one. If nothing meets on a ruler, pick "Doesn't have one".

Ink on canvas looks lighter and duller than on screen. These push it back. Watch the Preview.

Where a stitch sits beside bare canvas, the paint stops just past that stitch's own crossing instead of running halfway down the thread toward the next one, so a stitcher can always tell which crossings to stitch. At 100% the paint covers the whole crossing and stops right there; 0% paints halfway like a color-to-color edge. Not sure? Print the edge test below and pick what looks right on your canvas.

If a print lands off the threads
0,0

Each tap moves the print 1/400 inch (about 1/30 of a stitch on 13 mesh). Check my print below works out the exact nudge for you. Your nudge is remembered on this device.

Dress rehearsal without canvas: this prints your scanned canvas onto plain paper, placed exactly where the real canvas sits on the Carrier. Print it at 100% / Actual Size. Then load that paper the same way you load the Carrier and print your design PDF on it (same printer, same nudge). Ink should land on the printed threads the way it will on the real canvas. Threads print gray and the holes stay white, so it uses little ink.

Print it on plain paper, lay it on the Carrier and hold both up to a window. The crosshairs should sit on the four dots. If part of a print goes missing, turn off paper width detection in your printer settings.

Check my print

Something look off? Leave the printed canvas on the Carrier, scan it the same way, and load that scan here. The app measures where the ink landed on the threads and suggests a printer nudge if it's needed. Works best with the test pattern.

Ink spread: keep the scan of the blank canvas loaded (Scan step) when you check the printed one. The app compares the two and measures how far the paint runs past the design's outline on each side, then prints those edges shorter.
